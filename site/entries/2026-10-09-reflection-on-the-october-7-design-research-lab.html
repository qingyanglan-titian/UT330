Reflection on the October 7 Design Research Lab

October 9, 2026 · Week 7

In the October 7th design research lab, we learned about the ways to get information regarding experiences and decision-making process of various users through interviews. In our group, we considered the issue of coffee shops, namely, if it is feasible to prolong evening hours of operation for this type of business. There are two angles from which the issue can be analyzed: the angle of a coffee shop manager and the one of the client who comes to coffee shops to work in the evening.

Initially, I thought that increasing opening hours would be beneficial to customers of the coffee shop, especially those who study at university. Nevertheless, through interviews, I got a more complicated picture of the situation.

For the management, however, the operating hours have nothing to do with the preferences of the customers. Labor availability, labor costs, sales figures, and closing procedures have all played a role in determining operating hours. One good example would be an unusually busy evening due to a campus event happening close by. While there were more customers, the employees had to postpone the cleaning process and work overtime. This revealed to me that an increase in customer traffic does not mean that it will make everything easy and profitable.

As for the customer interview, this gave me another insight into the problem. According to the customer interviewed, he used coffee shops for studying, working, and sometimes even meeting friends. In selecting a coffee shop for an evening visit, the customer took into account such factors as closing time, availability of seating, noise, WiFi, availability of power sockets, and price. What is interesting about this is that the customer can spend hours in the coffee shop without buying anything there.

The other lesson that I learned was based on the interviewing process. It turned out that asking open-ended questions instead of giving recommendations on how to solve the issue is far more effective in terms of getting more details regarding behavior. Thus, instead of asking whether it makes sense to work longer hours at the coffee shop, I asked about their experience.

This practice helped me realize how closely the two fields are related, namely, research and design. One has to comprehend the different individuals and their constraints before coming up with an improvement. Something that seems to be obvious and simple might have multiple competing requirements.

Generally speaking, the session made me understand that interaction design does not involve working just with individuals. It is more about comprehending the bigger picture. Therefore, I would like to give more thought to how the stakeholders define the problem.
