Reflection on Product Team Sprint and Teamwork

October 9, 2026 · Week 7

As part of the Product Team Sprint, our team designed a pre-ordering app for coffee SpeedyBeans. The idea was to make an easy to use app which would allow users to place an order in 60 seconds. During the work on the project, I have gained a better understanding of what teamwork means in the context of interaction design and the difficulties that arise while converting individual visions to one unified user experience.

The main difficulty was the need for balance between simplicity and the number of features. Initially, the task of designing the coffee ordering app seemed very simple since almost everyone had experience with such services. Nevertheless, while talking about the interaction, we understood that there were many things to take into account. For instance, how to offer a possibility to customize your drink within 60 seconds? How to communicate information about waiting times or unavailability of products?

As there were five people on our team, each of us took responsibility for designing certain elements. That helped us perform faster but raised another issue that is consistent design. Indeed, everyone tends to have his or her personal opinion about design even if people collaborate and have common objectives. Thus, it became necessary to communicate in order to make sure that our separate screens form an integrated whole.

In regard to my personal experience, I have found the process of collaboration more exciting than I thought. Indeed, I am used to coming up with my own ideas and implementing them in my work independently. Collaboration has shown that it is essential to explain one’s decisions and possibly reconsider them.

An equally important lesson learned was that interaction design is about more than making visually beautiful screens. There should always be some purpose behind an action and the screens should assist the user to achieve their aim. Considering the whole ordering process allowed me to pay attention to the user’s needs instead of designing the appearance of separate elements.

In general, this sprint helped me see the connection between individual creative process and group decision-making. Effective teamwork does not always mean reaching consensus in every issue. Instead, it means effective communication and mutual understanding of the task at hand. I believe that such experience will be useful for future projects, particularly complex ones.
